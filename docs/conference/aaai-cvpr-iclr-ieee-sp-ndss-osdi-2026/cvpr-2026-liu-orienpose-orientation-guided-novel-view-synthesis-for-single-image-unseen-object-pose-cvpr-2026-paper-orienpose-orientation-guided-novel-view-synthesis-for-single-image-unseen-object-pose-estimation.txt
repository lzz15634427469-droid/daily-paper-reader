# Abstract 

Estimating the 3D pose of unseen objects from a sin-gle image remains a fundamental yet challenging prob-lem in computer vision, especially under a CAD model-free setting. Pioneering attempts address this issue by matching templates generated through Novel View Syn-thesis (NVS), which essentially aims to learn the geo-metric transformation from a reference to a target view. While promising, these methods can only approximate this transformation under pixel-level supervision, as the starting orientation remains undefined. In the absence of explicit geometric constraints to verify the correctness of the predicted transformation, existing methods often synthesize novel views with geometry-distorted struc-tures or severely blurred local textures, leading to un-reliable template matching and suboptimal pose estima-tion results. To this end, we propose OrienPose , an un-

Specifically, we intro-duce the Orientation-Aware Guidance, which explicitly injects object orientation cues into the reference latent embedding to enhance orientation awareness during viewpoint transformation. We also introduce an orien-tation consistency loss that supervises viewpoint trans-formation at the geometric level, establishing sufficient supervision for explicit and geometry-consistent trans-formation guidance beyond pixel-level similarity. This loss justifies estimating the reference orientation rather than using its ground-truth pose, thereby ensuring the alignment of coordinate domains between the injected and supervised priors. Extensive experiments demon-strate that OrienPose achieves state-of-the-art perfor-mance in single-view unseen object pose estimation and impressive robustness to image degradations. Estimating an object’s 3D rotation from a single or several images is fundamental in applications such as robotics [26, 36], autonomous driving [8, 40], and AR/VR [7, 14]. Early works, such as regression-[16, 44, 50] or PnP-based [29–31] approaches, have achieved impressive estimation accuracy, most of which, however, operate at instance- or category-level and can-not generalize to unseen objects without retraining. To improve generalization performance, render-and-compare (R&C) methods [28] leverage an object-agnostic matcher for template matching, achieving strong cross-category generalization. However, render-ing dense view templates still relies on a known CAD model of the object, narrowing their real-world appli-cation. Recent efforts alleviate the CAD reliance by capturing rather than rendering a set of posed refer-ence images as templates [22], or reconstructing an ex-plicit [13, 33] or implicit [19, 25] 3D model from these references. While model-free generalization was achieved, their performance degrades significantly in a sparse or single-view setting, leaving single-image esti-mation a challenging open problem. Benefiting from the remarkable capacity of zero-shot image generation, recent works have introduced gen-erative models into novel view synthesis (NVS), moti-vating a more flexible “generate-and-compare” (G&C) paradigm compared to the conventional “render-and-compare” (R&C) scheme. Instead of rendering from CAD models, G&C methods [27, 42] generate novel view templates directly from a single reference image and its corresponding target views, offering a promising solution for single-image unseen object pose estimation. Despite the recent progress, NVS is essentially a ill-posed transformation problem, i.e., learning the ge-ometric transformation “ ? + ∆ R =? ” between refer-ence and target views without a defined starting ori-entation. Most of the NVS networks [5, 27, 42] rely solely on pixel-level supervision (e.g., L2 loss), which provides no explicit geometric constraints to ensure transformation correctness. As a result, the generated novel views often suffer from geometry-distorted struc-tures and blurred textures, leading to unreliable template matching and suboptimal pose estimation. The latest work, NOPE [27], has noticed this issue and introduces explicit rotation information from the reference to the target view, generating novel views with roughly correct contours. Nevertheless, we observe that the intrinsic object orientation serves as a degradation-robust geo-metric cue that is crucial for defining the transforma-tion. Ignoring this orientation information results in cur-rent NVS methods producing geometry-inconsistent and texture-blurred novel views, ultimately failing to allevi-ate the ill-posed transformation problem. To overcome these limitations, we propose Orien-Pose , an unseen object pose estimation framework via orientation-guided NVS from a single image. Following the G&C paradigm, we predict the pose of unseen ob-jects through template matching, but with an explicit ge-ometric formulation. As illustrated in Fig. 1 (top), since the viewpoint transformation in NVS is inherently ill-posed , we introduce the Orientation-Aware Guidance to provide explicit starting and ending orientation to form a defined geometric transformation. It explicitly guides the viewpoint transformation by injecting the probabilis-tic distribution of object orientation priors (via an orien-tation predictor) into the latent embedding of the ref-erence view. This process establishes a geometrically meaningful initialization for NVS, effectively mitigating the ambiguity of the transformation. To further com-pensate for the lack of explicit geometric constraints ,we design an orientation consistency loss that supervises the transformation at the geometric level, enforcing a geometry-aligned synthesis. As shown in Fig. 1 (bot-tom), our approach not only maintains accurate object contours but also preserves fine geometric structures in synthesized views, enhancing both template matching reliability and pose estimation accuracy. A natural question arises: “Why not use the orienta-tion estimator directly for pose prediction?” The main challenge lies in the mismatch between the estimator’s semantic coordinate frame and the object’s canonical one, which makes its predictions unreliable for abso-lute pose recovery. Instead, the estimator is employed to model consistent orientation distributions in Euler-angle space, providing stable orientation priors even un-der large viewpoint variations. This design enables the NVS process to establish a defined transformation guid-ance (i.e., Oref +∆ R = Osyn ), where orientation injec-tion at the beginning and orientation supervision at the end form a geometrically consistent learning cycle. Our contributions are as follows: • We propose an unseen object 3D pose estimation framework based on orientation-guided novel view synthesis, which requires only a single image without the need for CAD models. • We inject explicit orientation priors into reference la-tent features via an Orientation-Aware Guidance to al-leviate the challenge of ill-posed transformation, as well as an orientation consistency loss to handle the lack of explicit geometric constraints during NVS. • Our method achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks, demonstrating impressive gen-eralization to unseen objects and considerable robust-ness to challenging scenarios with occlusion and blur. 26814 2. Related work 

2.1. RGB-only unseen object pose estimation 

Model-based methods . In contrast to instance- [30, 44] or category-level methods [21, 32, 39], model-based methods often follow a “render-and-compare” pipeline to achieve unseen object pose estimation. Assuming the presence of a CAD model, these methods render dense view templates from it, which are then matched to the query image to yield the pose. To enhance generaliza-tion to unseen objects, template matching in pixel or latent feature space [1, 18, 28] is developed, achieving strong cross-category generalization. However, the as-sumption of a known CAD model significantly narrows their applicability in real-world settings. 

Model-free methods . To mitigate the CAD reliance, one pioneering work [22] is to capture posed refer-ence images as templates rather than render them from a CAD model. Follow-up works instead reconstruct an explicit or implicit 3D representation of the object from these references, such as [13, 19, 33, 34, 45], from which 2D-3D correspondences are established to yield the object pose via PnP solvers [43]. [12] developed analytical solutions for precise complex motion estima-tion [9–11], their proof of degeneracy cases addressed the bottlenecks of weak geometric configurations, re-sulting in superior accuracy and a 2.5x speedup over ex-isting methods. Despite this, their performance degrades when only sparse-view references are provided, suffers from incomplete reconstruction due to unreliable match-ing between views. 

Single-view unseen object estimation. Unseen object estimation from only a single image is rather challeng-ing due to significant ill-posedness with limited priors. PIZZA [6] directly regresses the relative pose between the query and reference images. MicKey [2] instead solves pose via a metric keypoint matching pipeline. RelPose [47] and its improved version RelPose++ [20] model the relative pose of two views as a conditional probability distribution, from which the pose with the highest probability is regarded as the best estimate. Follow-up work [48] enhances them by transforming images into 3D latent representations to achieve 3D awareness. While effective, these regression, match-ing and probability-based methods tend to learn more category-specific priors, which prove to be suboptimal in unseen object pose estimation [27, 35] Recently, NOPE [27] introduced generative models to “generate” novel view templates from a single ref-erence view instead of “render” or “capture”, achieving impressive generalization to unseen objects and state-of-the-art accuracy. A concurrent work [35] enhances the robustness to large viewpoint variation via a two-sided generation. However, learning to control the camera viewpoint efficiently is inherently challenging for these methods under pixel-level supervision alone, since they overlook the rich source of information provided by ob-ject orientation. 

2.2. Single-image novel view synthesis 

Generative models address the single-view NVS prob-lem by enforcing multi-view or 3D geometric consis-tency [5]. Multi-view consistency is achieved through stochastic [42] or ray conditioning [49], 3D-aware fea-ture attention [23], and epipolar constraints [37], while 3D geometric consistency is maintained via learning ge-ometric priors in feature volumes [3] or video diffusion models [17, 38]. Despite promising zero-shot gener-alization, these approaches overlook object orientation consistency, leading to geometry misaligned synthesis. In contrast, our framework forms an explicit transfor-mation guidance that injects and supervises orientations, achieving geometrically consistent novel view synthesis and reliable pose estimation. 

# 3. Method 

Given a single reference image Iref , our goal is to estimate the 3D (rotation) pose of the object in a query image Iqry . Following a “generate-and-compare” paradigm, we introduce an orientation-guided novel view synthesis (NVS) network (see Fig. 2) to “generate” multi-view templates from the input reference image and a set of viewpoints (or poses) conditioning the gener-ation. To achieve orientation awareness during NVS, an orientation-aware guidance (OAG) module is con-structed to inject the orientation distribution of the ob-ject in Iref into its latent embedding. Additionally, we develop an orientation-consistency loss to further guide the learning of the NVS network for a better control of viewpoint transformation. Those generated templates are then “compared” to Iqry , see Fig. 3, of which the pose corresponding to that most similar to Iqry is our estimate. Details are provided as follows. 

3.1. Orientation-Aware Guidance 

OAG injects the object orientation of the reference im-age into its latent representation to enable orientation-aware guidance for NVS. To avoid the texture distor-tions and generative artifacts noted in [27], this injection is performed entirely in the latent space by projecting both the reference image and its estimated orientation into compact embeddings. The image and orientation embeddings are obtained using a pretrained encoder and an MLP, respectively. 3.2. Orientation consistency loss 

We further enhance our NVS network by constructing an orientation consistency loss (OCL) during learning the control of viewpoint transformation. OCL consists of both the orientation loss LOC and the traditional pixel-level L2 loss, expressed as 

L = λ1L2 + λ2LOC , (2) where λ1 and λ2 are weights for each loss, respectively. In contrast to using a cross-entropy [41], we build 

LOC on the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the predicted and ground-truth distributions for each ori-entation angle, which proves to be more effective for su-pervising the distribution learning, as 

LOC =μ1DKL (P αgt , P αref ) + μ2DKL (P θ gt, P θ ref )+ μ3DKL (P ω gt, P ω ref ),

(3) where μi, i = 1, 2, and 3 are weight factors. We also adopt L2 loss to achieve pixel-level supervision.

# 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental setup 

Datasets and Metrics . We used the same training dataset in [27] for training our network, which contains rendered images from 3D models in ShapeNet [4]. Two test sets were constructed, including 10 categories from ShapeNet and 5 instances from the real-world dataset NAVI [15]. Please note that all instances and categories in the test sets are never seen during training, facilitat-ing fair performance evaluation of unseen object estima-tion. Following the protocol in [27], 85% of the refer-ence images were randomly chosen with an azimuth in 

|| azimuth || ≤ 45 ◦ compared to the query, while the remaining 15% were chosen from outside this range. More details have been provided in the Supplementary material . We used ACC 30 and Median pose error as in [24, 27] to characterize the estimation performance. 

Comparative Methods. We compared OrienPose with state-of-the-art single-image unseen-level meth-ods requiring no CAD models, including the “generate-and-compare” method NOPE [27], regression-based method PIZZA [6], keypoint-based method MicKey [2], and probability-based methods RelPose [47] and Rel-Pose++ [20], which required only two views (one ref-erence and a query view, respectively) to align with our setting. We also implemented a strong baseline by replacing the NVS network in NOPE with a pow-erful diffusion-based NVS Free3D [49]. Since Free3D leverages different cues (i.e., 3D priors across multi-ple views) in contrast to the object orientation used in our model, comparison between them provides more in-sight about the effectiveness of the object orientation. 4.2. Comparison results 

Results on ShapeNet . As shown in Tab. 1, OnePose++ and BoxDreamer only achieve average performance among SoTA methods, even given multiple views, which significantly degrade in our sparse-view set-ting. In contrast, RelPose, RelPose++, and PIZZA have shown high consistency across categories, since they learned generalizable priors while modeling the pose distribution. However, the lack of leverage of 3D pri-ors limits their performance to a similar level of es-timation accuracy. MicKey improves slightly in met-rics compared to regression-based PIZZA, despite the weak texture of the object, even with explicit predic-tion of 3D keypoints. Unlike using deterministic mod-els in the above methods, NOPE leverages a generative model to implicitly model the viewpoint transition with 3D awareness, achieving the previous SoTA results, with 52.3% and 27 .7◦ for ACC 30 and Median, respectively. It should be noted that no obvious gain has been ob-served for Free3D compared to NOPE. This may be at-tributed to the diffusion model, which changes the tex-ture or invents incorrect details in generated views, as indicated in [27]. In contrast, since the stronger orien-tation cue has been injected for better novel viewpoint control, our orientation-aware OrientPose outperforms NOPE by a large margin, i.e., an average gain of 7.3%

in ACC 30 and a reduction of 7.3◦ in Median, setting a new SoTA in the single-image unseen-level task without CAD models. The visualization results, as shown in Fig. 4, further confirm our findings. Due to the lack of explicit geomet-ric modeling, NOPE only maintains a coarse-level trans-formation consistency between generated novel views, focusing mostly on the shape contour preservation with-out sufficient details. Accordingly, NOPE performs more effectively for objects with anisotropic shapes, see 

bottle, clock, mug, and washer (with an obvious vi-sual boundary between the white and red textures on it) , whereas it often yields flipped poses for those with isotropic structures, which raises ambiguity without ad-ditional details. In contrast, our OrienPose, guided by orientation-aware injection and supervision, achieves better viewpoint control with highly consistent geomet-ric details preserved during view transformation, en-abling more discriminative in difficult objects for NOPE and thus superior performances. Considering the space limitation, the latent features generated by NOPE and ours demonstrate the preservation of contour and detail, as shown in the Supplementary Material .

4.4. Limitations 

We report the results in a more challenging scenario with large viewpoint changes between the reference and query views. The viewpoint interval was chosen ran-domly within a larger range [45 ◦, 90 ◦] in the NAVI test set. As shown in Fig. 7, while noticeable errors can be observed, OrienPose still achieves promising results compared to those of NOPE, which tends to fail with almost random guesses. Further research will be con-ducted to learn more about priors in multiple views or the underlying 3D geometry of the object, in order to better control the large viewpoint transformation. 

# 5. Conclusion 

We propose a 3D object pose estimation method, Orien-Pose, following a “generate-and-compare” paradigm, which requires only a single reference image and can di-rectly generalize to unseen objects without retraining or the input of a CAD model. An orientation-aware novel view synthesis framework is developed to learn better control of the ill-posed viewpoint transformation, where explicit orientation priors are injected into the reference latent embedding via orientation-aware guidance. Ad-ditionally, an orientation consistency loss is constructed to provide additional explicit geometric constraints dur-ing NVS. The superiority of OrienPose has been demon-strated by extensive results, which show impressive gen-eralizability to unseen objects and considerable robust-ness to significant image degradations, advancing pose estimation toward more challenging open-set scenarios. 26820 References
