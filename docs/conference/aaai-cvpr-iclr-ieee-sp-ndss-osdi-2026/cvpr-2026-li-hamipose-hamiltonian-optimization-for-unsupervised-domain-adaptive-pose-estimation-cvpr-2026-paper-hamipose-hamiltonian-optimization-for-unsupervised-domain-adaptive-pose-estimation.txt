## Abstract 

Unsupervised domain adaptation (UDA) for pose estima-tion promises transfer from synthetic to real domains but often suffers instability under domain shift. Prior work at-tributes this deterioration to gradient interference between source supervision and target consistency. This conflict is distinct in pose estimation, where sparse and heterogeneous supervision signals cause gradients to be highly sensitive to small localization errors and lead to unstable updates. To address these challenges, we propose HamiPose, a Hamil-tonian optimization framework that transports decoupled and confidence-calibrated gradients within a unified geom-etry to mitigate instability. HamiPose first refines gradient interaction through keypointwise geometry decomposition, orthogonally projecting target gradients to preserve non-conflicting component. Channelwise gated alignment then calibrates the parallel component with confidence and align-ment, producing decoupled, confidence-calibrated gradients. These gradients are advanced by a Hamiltonian optimizer with a symplectic integrator, providing controlled momentum that stabilizes updates. Extensive experiments demonstrate that HamiPose achieves state-of-the-art performance in UDA pose estimation while maintains strong performance under domain generalization settings.

Pose estimation is a fundamental problem in computer vi-sion with applications in human–computer interaction, aug-mented reality, and video understanding [4, 5]. However, building large, labeled real-world pose datasets is costly and time-consuming, which limits the scalability of supervised methods. Synthetic datasets provide a practical alternative: they can be generated at scale with precise annotations and controlled variability [23, 42]. This data landscape motivates the adoption of Unsupervised Domain Adaptation (UDA) as a practical solution. By transferring knowledge from la-

Early work on UDA pose estimation emphasized repre-sentation alignment through adversarial training and mo-ment matching to reduce the distribution gap between source and the target domains [14, 18]. Subsequent studies moved toward the prediction space by enforcing agreement with pseudo heatmaps and consistency regularization, while also introducing source-free and test-time protocols to enhance practicality [18, 24, 28, 29]. However, aligning features or predictions alone cannot fully resolve how multiple train-ing signals interact during optimization. This limitation has motivated a shift of attention from global alignment to the optimization dynamics, revealing that performance degrada-tion under domain shift often stems from gradient conflict between the source-supervised objective and the target con-sistency gradients [6, 9, 25, 31]. Even minor spatial offsets can flip gradient directions and destabilize updates, an effect that intensifies near sharp responses and is linked to optimization instability [8]. In addition, the quality of pseudo heatmaps varies with occlusion, truncation, and motion blur, which makes supervision reliability fluctuate across joints and propagates as label noise during adaptation [18, 29]. Therefore, conflict is heterogeneous across keypoints, with a few difficult joints dominating disagreement while others remain consistent. These two properties, sparsity and het-erogeneity, induce oscillatory parameter updates that hinder stable convergence and weaken cross-domain generalization. These observations motivate a dynamics-preserving for-mulation that stabilizes parameter evolution under sparse and heterogeneous supervision. Hamiltonian dynamics meets this need by modeling systems that exhibit oscilla-tion and long-horizon evolution, where conserved quantities and geometry-preserving trajectories curb artificial energy drift and mitigate numerical overshoot during prolonged integration. Symplectic integrators are designed for this setting and are known to preserve the qualitative stability of oscillatory motion without sacrificing efficiency. Car-rying this perspective to learning, we treat optimization as geometry-aware transport on an energy landscape, with the loss acting as potential energy and a data-driven metric inducing kinetic energy, so that parameter updates evolve along structure-preserving flows with controlled momen-tum. Therefore, a Hamiltonian view provides the required dynamics-preserving foundation and supports a geometry-consistent optimization paradigm that stabilizes adaptation under sparse and heterogeneous supervision. To realize this idea, we introduce HamiPose, which mod-els the optimization process as a Hamiltonian system. Hami-Pose first refines gradient interaction at the detection head, where domain shift is most pronounced and pseudo signals are most variable. For each keypoint, the target gradient is decomposed into components that are orthogonal and parallel to the source direction, and the nonconflicting or-thogonal component is preserved to avoid destructive cancel-lation across heterogeneous joints. The parallel component is calibrated by channelwise gated alignment that combines geometric agreement with teacher confidence, which sup-presses unreliable pseudo signals while retaining informa-tive ones. A simple warmup on the alignment sharpness avoids premature hard filtering and gradually increases se-lectivity as estimates improve. The resulting decoupled and confidence-calibrated gradients are then transported by a Hamiltonian optimizer that uses a symplectic integrator to supply controlled momentum. This mechanism smooths high-frequency oscillations induced by sparse and peaky supervision, maintains first-order efficiency, and improves long-horizon stability. Overall, these elements produce a sta-ble and geometry-consistent optimization procedure that re-duces gradient conflict, enhances convergence under limited labels, and strengthens cross-domain generalization. The main contributions of our work are summarized as follows: • We introduce HamiPose, a geometry-consistent Hamilto-nian optimization framework for UDA pose estimation that mitigates gradient interference arising from sparse, localized supervision and heterogeneous pseudo-labels. • We derive a conflict-mitigated update rule by orthogonally decomposing per-keypoint gradients and gating the paral-lel component with channel-level alignment. A data-driven diagonal metric and symplectic integration then transport the updates in a unified geometry for stable convergence. • We validate the effectiveness of HamiPose across multiple UDA pose estimation benchmarks and it achieves state-of-the-art performance. In addition, it delivers competitive results on domain generalization benchmarks. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. Unsupervised Domain Adaptive Pose Estima-tion 

Unsupervised domain adaptive (UDA) pose estimation trans-fers supervision from labeled source data to unlabeled target data by combining input or style alignment, temporal or consistency priors, and teacher-student learning. Classical works such as CC-SSL [26], RegDA [14], and TransPar [10] encourage cross-domain consistency through shared architec-tures or feature extractors, while Mean Teacher [33] variants refine pseudo labels to stabilize training [15, 18, 22]. Re-cent work shifts from generic alignment to setting-aware robustness. Source-free adaptation removes dependence on source data during adaptation and is attractive when data sharing is restricted [29]. Dual-teacher strategies and pho-tometric priors improve transfer in low-light scenes [1]. Se-vere motion blur is mitigated by coupling RGB supervision with event cues to restore spatial structure under fast mo-tion [19]. Occlusion-resilient designs curb error propaga-tion from noisy pseudo labels when joints are missing or truncated [7]. Although distinct from UDA, domain gen-eralization in 3D pose offers complementary gains by en-couraging invariance through augmentation and optimiza-tion [30]. Across these directions, a recurring challenge is the interaction between source-driven and target-driven up-dates. Misaligned gradients can destabilize optimization and slow convergence, motivating approaches that diagnose and regulate gradient interactions during adaptation [6, 25]. 

2.2. Gradient Matching for Domain Adaptation 

Aligning optimization dynamics across domains through gradient criteria is a central principle for UDA. Pioneer-ing methods methods reduce cross-domain gradient discrep-ancy to stabilize training and improve transfer, while gradi-ent–distribution alignment matches feature–gradient statis-13857 tics to tighten generalization [6, 9]. Recent work extends this view to large models and distributed settings by aligning both directions and magnitudes. Prompt-based UDA aligns per-objective gradients with an added norm regularizer and improves stability in vision–language adaptation, and fed-erated matching provides a coordination signal when data cannot be shared [27, 31, 36]. Evidence from cross-domain face anti-spoofing links gradient alignment to flatter min-ima under shift, and semantic conditioning further refines matching when labels or categories are available [2, 12, 21]. Complementary to these criteria on instantaneous updates, Gradual Domain Adaptation via Gradient Flow aligns the tra-jectory of optimization by transporting the source toward the target through intermediate domains with preserved labels, which yields smoother steps and reduces abrupt changes in descent directions [41]. Unlike classification, pose estima-tion optimizes structured, spatially peaked heatmap outputs. Gradients are highly sparse and per-keypoint heterogeneous due to occlusion, truncation, and viewpoint changes. Pseudo-label noise compounds this by amplifying conflicting compo-nents for a subset of joints, making cosine-similarity drops local and intermittent. Therefore, we propose to accommo-date joint-wise inconsistency and selectively regulate target updates rather than enforcing global agreement. 

2.3. Gradient Operations for Domain Generaliza-tion 

Domain generalization (DG) aims to remain robust on un-seen domains without target labels. Operating in gradi-ent space helps control cross-domain interference. Beyond projection, gradient-statistics matching aligns higher-order structure across environments. Fishr matches per-parameter gradient variances as an empirical Fisher proxy and reduces domain-specific drift [32]. Sharpness-aware methods pro-vide a complementary view. SAM seeks flatter minima by optimizing a local worst-case neighborhood and has in-spired DG variants that couple flatness with cross-domain goals [8, 39]. Projection-based surgery removes opposing components to increase agreement across domains, with conflict-aware variants integrating adversarial and task gra-dients toward conflict-free directions [25, 38]. Gradient matching further strengthens DG by minimizing the gap be-tween clean and perturbed objectives [35], while gradient discrepancy minimization coordinates multiple sources in federated DG [36]. Recent work explores training-time an-nealing and Pareto-guided searches over gradient directions to promote inter-domain agreement [3].

## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Datasets and Evaluation Metrics 

Datasets. We evaluate our method on three human pose datasets and three hand pose datasets, encompassing both synthetic and real-world domains. For human pose estima-tion, we use SURREAL [34] as the synthetic source domain, while Human3.6M [13] and Leeds Sports Pose (LSP) [17] serve as real-world target domains. For hand pose estimation, we adopt the Rendered Hand Pose Dataset (RHD) [42] as the synthetic source domain, with Hand-3D-Studio (H3D) [40] and FreiHand [43] as real-world target domains. More de-tails are presented in Appendix. 

Metrics. We use the Percentage of Correct Keypoints (PCK) metric, a standard evaluation protocol in pose estima-tion. Specifically, we report PCK@0.05, which calculates the percentage of predicted keypoints that fall within 5% of the image size from the ground truth. For the 16-keypoint hu-man body skeleton, we break down the results into six major regions: Shoulder (Sld), Elbow (Elb), Wrist, Hip, Knee, and Ankle. For the 21-keypoint hand skeleton, we report part-based accuracy on the Metacarpophalangeal joints (MCP), Proximal Interphalangeal joints (PIP), Distal Interphalangeal joints (DIP), and Fingertips (Fin).

## 5. Conclusion 

This paper reframes UDA for pose estimation as an optimiza-tion consistency problem under sparse and heterogeneous su-pervision and introduces HamiPose to resolve it. HamiPose uses a unified data driven metric to evaluate source–target in-teraction, performs keypoint level conflict filtering by decom-posing target gradients into parallel and orthogonal compo-nents with confidence gating, and integrates updates through a symplectic scheme that provides momentum while sup-pressing oscillation. Extensive experiments on UDA pose estimation benchmarks verify its effectiveness, and it also performs strongly on domain generalization tasks. 13863 6. Acknowledgments
