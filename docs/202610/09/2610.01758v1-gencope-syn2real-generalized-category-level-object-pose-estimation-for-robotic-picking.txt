## Abstract 

Category-level object pose estimation (COPE), capable of generalizing to intra-class unknown objects, has become a core technique for robotic 3D scene under-standing. However, existing COPE methods still require labor-intensive recollection of real-world training data for novel object categories, which limits their scalability in practical applications. This paper aims to achieve synthetic-to-real (Syn2Real) generalized COPE, where a model is trained solely on rendered synthetic data and directly generalized to real-world deployments. The central challenge lies in the significant domain gap between synthetic and real-world data, particularly in texture appearance. To address this, we aim to enhance domain generalization by learning domain-invariant representations that capture semantic commonalities among objects within the same category. We introduce 2D and 3D semantic consis-tency constraints to reduce the sensitivity of feature encoders to domain-specific features. In addition, we propose an end-to-end pose regression framework that per-forms 2D-3D cross consistency learning, leveraging dense cross-modality fusion to further refine pose estimation. Since simplicity and effectiveness are essential for real-world robotic deployment, our model operates exclusively on global features, yielding a highly lightweight and efficient architecture. Extensive experiments on the REAL275 and Wild6D benchmarks, as well as real-world robotic manipulation scenes, show superior Syn2Real generalization performance of our paradigm.

Nine-degrees-of-freedom (9DoF) object pose estimation, which aims to estimate the 3DoF translation, 3DoF rotation, and 3DoF size of an object in the camera coordinate system [1, 2, 3, 4], is a fundamental problem in robotic 3D scene understanding and manipulation [5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10]. Early studies primarily focused on instance-level pose estimation [11, 12, 13, 14, 15], where models are restricted to objects seen during training. Alternatively, category-level object pose estimation (COPE) has emerged as a promising direction [16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22], as it enables generalization to intra-class unknown objects without relying on explicit prior information such as CAD models or reference views. Despite the impressive generalization capability of category-level approaches across unknown in-stances within the same category, existing methods still require labor-intensive collection and an-notation of real-world training data for each novel object category [23, 24, 25, 26]. This require-ment significantly hinders scalability and limits their practicality in large-scale robotic applications [27, 28, 29, 30]. In contrast, recent advances in rendering technology have made large-scale synthetic data readily available, with accurate pose annotations obtained at negligible cost. Motivated by this observation, this paper aims to achieve Syn2Real domain-generalized COPE, referred to as GenCOPE, where models trained purely on synthetic data can be directly deployed in real world. The central challenge in GenCOPE lies in the significant domain gap between synthetic and real-world data. Through an analysis of the rendered synthetic data used for object pose estimation [2], we observe that the most pronounced discrepancy arises from texture appearance, as illustrated in Fig. 1. However, existing methods primarily rely on feature extractors [31, 32] that emphasize fine-grained image features [33, 34, 35], which inherently encode substantial texture information while failing to explicitly capture domain-invariant properties shared between synthetic and real-world data. Consequently, the learned representations become overly sensitive to texture variations, making them suboptimal for robust Syn2Real domain-generalized pose estimation. To address the aforementioned challenge, we argue that although intra-class unknown objects exhibit substantial texture discrepancies between synthetic and real-world domains, their 2D and 3D semantic structures remain largely consistent. For instance, at the 2D level, objects such as bottles typically consist of semantic parts including a cap, a neck, and a base container . Similarly, at the 3D level, their geometric composition can be abstracted as a small neck cylinder, a transitional cone, and a large bottom cylinder . Based on this observation, we posit that explicitly incorporating semantic knowledge as constraints during 2D and 3D feature encoding can encourage encoders to focus on domain-invariant semantic cues, thereby suppressing the adverse effects of domain-specific features. An overview of the proposed GenCOPE is illustrated in Fig. 1. Given a segmented RGB image and its corresponding point cloud (obtained via depth map back-projection) as inputs, we first perform feature embedding using the proposed 2D/3D semantic consistency encoders, followed by a 2D-3D cross consistency learning module and an end-to-end pose decouple decoder. During training, semantic consistency is enforced by leveraging semantic supervision derived from a pretrained large language model [36] and the CLIP text encoder [37], which aligns the 2D and 3D feature representations in a shared semantic space. The resulting semantically consistent features are then fused through a 2D-3D cross transformer-based module to perform cross-consistency learning [38]. Finally, the fused features are concatenated and fed into a pose decouple decoder to regress the full 9DoF object pose. To prevent feature extractors from overemphasizing fine-grained texture-dependent features while maintaining computational efficiency, we adopt the most lightweight backbones based on ResNet18 [39] and PointNet [40] as the 2D and 3D encoders, respectively, and operate exclusively on global features throughout the pipeline. This design is deliberately adopted to jointly enhance domain generalization robustness while meeting the real-time requirements of practical robotic manipulation scenarios. Overall, our main contributions are as follows: (1) We propose a simple yet effective framework for Syn2Real domain-generalized COPE, explicitly designed for real-world robotic deployment. The framework operates purely on global features from most lightweight backbones, avoiding dense local texture correspondences, making it highly efficient under resource-constrained settings. (2) We introduce semantic consistency encoders in both 2D and 3D spaces to learn semantic-invariant features shared across intra-class objects, alleviating the severe gap between synthetic and real-world domains. (3) We propose an end-to-end pose estimation pipeline based on 2D-3D cross consistency learning, featuring a cross-modal fusion network and a pose decouple decoder, enabling mutual reinforcement across modalities for more accurate and robust pose estimation. 22 Related Work 

Fully-Supervised COPE Methods. NOCS [2] introduces a normalized object coordinate space formulation and establishes two widely adopted benchmarks that advance the research of COPE methods. To address substantial shape variability among intra-class objects, SPD [41] first leverages category-level CAD model libraries to extract shape priors offline and subsequently reconstructs NOCS representations through shape prior deformation for pose estimation. This paradigm has inspired a series of follow-up approaches that explicitly incorporate shape priors [42, 43, 44, 45]. Despite their effectiveness in modeling intra-class shape variability, prior-based approaches rely on manually constructed category-level CAD model libraries, which incur substantial human effort. The limitation aforementioned has motivated the development of prior-free alternatives [46, 47, 48, 34, 21, 49, 50, 51, 52]. FS-Net [47] proposes a 3D graph convolution-based network and performs decoupled pose regression. GPV-Pose [48] enhances the learning of pose-sensitive features via geometry-guided point-wise voting. Beyond these depth-only approaches, several RGB-D methods [34, 53] jointly leverage appearance and geometric cues. SecondPose [54] introduces DINOv2 [55] to provide SE(3)-consistent semantic features for pose estimation. GCE-Pose [56] enhances COPE under partial visibility by reconstructing category-level global context via semantic shape reconstruction and fusing it with local RGB-D observations. CleanPose [52] mitigates data bias by integrating causal inference with front-door adjustment and residual-based knowledge distillation from 3D foundation models. Although these fully-supervised methods achieve competitive performance, their dependence on extensive real-world data annotation makes them difficult to scale to novel object categories. 

Label-Efficient COPE Methods. To alleviate the scalability bottleneck caused by extensive real-world data annotation, recent studies have explored label-efficient COPE [57, 58, 59, 60, 33, 61, 62, 63, 64, 65]. CPPF [57] introduces a category-level point pair feature voting formulation that enables pose estimation in the wild without dense supervision. SAR-Net [58] investigates object shape alignment by directly matching the observed point cloud with shape prior, reducing the reliance on real-world training data. DPDN [33] designs a domain-generalized learning framework based on parallel deep prior deformation. UDA-COPE [61] leverages a teacher-student self-supervised learning scheme to achieve domain adaptation. Further, TTA-COPE [62] proposes a test-time adaptation strategy that first learns from labeled synthetic data and then adapts the pretrained model online to real-world data during inference. More recently, DiffusionNOCS [64] adopts diffusion models to predict dense canonical maps for pose estimation, demonstrating strong robustness to symmetry and effective Syn2Real generalization. Diff9D [65] directly formulates COPE as a diffusion-based generative process, enabling Syn2Real generalization without requiring 3D shape priors. Although existing methods perform well, they do not explicitly model the consistent properties shared between synthetic and real-world data. In contrast, this paper analyzes the domain-invariant characteristics across synthetic and real domains and explicitly incorporates semantic knowledge as constraints during both 2D and 3D feature encoding. This design encourages encoders to focus on domain-invariant semantic cues, alleviating the adverse impact of domain-specific information. 

## 3 Workflow of GenCOPE 

Task definition : GenCOPE aims to predict the 6DoF object pose (consisting of 3DoF translation and 3DoF rotation) and 3DoF object size (axes lengths of oriented bounding box) of intra-class unknown objects in real world using the model trained only on synthetic dataset. 

3.1 Overview 

GenCOPE takes a single RGB-D image as input. The target object is first segmented using Mask R-CNN [66] to obtain an object-centric RGB image I [67, 68]. The corresponding segmentation mask is then applied to crop the depth map, which is back-projected to generate a point cloud P

with 1024 sampled points. This preprocessing follows the standard pipeline adopted by most prior works [42, 52, 65]. Subsequently, I and P are processed by dedicated 2D and 3D encoders equipped with semantic consistency optimization ( applied only during training ) to extract global semantic consistency features FC-2D and FC-3D. The resulting features are then fused by a proposed 2D-3D cross transformer network, which explicitly models cross-modal consistency. 3.2 2D and 3D Semantic Consistency Encoders 

To introduce intra-class semantic commonalities into RGB image and point cloud encoders in a simple yet effective manner, we leverage semantic optimization generated by the large language model (LLM) Gemini-3 [36], as illustrated in (a) and (b) of Fig. 2. Specifically, we define a semantic query template: Output the common 2D/3D semantic information of a class of objects enclosed in parentheses in the following format: “All cls. consist of [n] features: [f1], . . . , [ fn]”, where cls. 

denotes the segmented object class, and the number of features [ n] as well as the semantic descriptors [f1]-[ fn] are inferred by the LLM. To enhance the diversity of semantic descriptions and mitigate the impact of uncertainty, we prompt the LLM to generate ten distinct semantic descriptions for each object class and randomly select one description for each training sample. Since this semantic consistency optimization is applied only in the training phase, the ground-truth class label cls. for each image is directly available. For instance, when cls. corresponds to bottle , a representative 2D semantic description is “All bottles consist of three features: a cap, a neck, and a base container” ,while the corresponding 3D semantic description is “All bottles consist of three features: a small neck cylinder, a transition cone, and a large bottom cylinder” . Additional LLM-generated semantic examples for different object categories are provided in the Appendix A. The generated 2D and 3D semantic descriptions are encoded using the CLIP pretrained text encoder [37], producing semantic feature embeddings Ftext -2D ∈ Rb×512 and Ftext -3D ∈ Rb×512 , where b

denotes the batch size. These semantic embeddings are then employed to optimize the global features encoding processes of the 2D and 3D encoders [39, 40], respectively, resulting in semantically consistent representations FC-2D ∈ Rb×512 and FC-3D ∈ Rb×512 . By explicitly encouraging the encoders to attend to intra-class semantic invariances, the proposed optimization suppresses sensitivity to discrepancies between synthetic and real-world domains, improving domain generalization ability. We consider a batch consisting of b images and b text descriptions, and let F iC-2D and F itext -2D denote the 2D feature of the i-th image and the semantic feature of the i-th text, respectively. 3.3 2D-3D Cross Consistency Learning for Pose Regression 

To effectively align semantic representations across modalities and enable robust pose estimation, we introduce a 2D-3D cross consistency learning framework coupled with an end-to-end pose decouple decoder. As illustrated in (c) and (d) of Fig. 2, the proposed design jointly fuses semantically consistent features FC-2D and FC-3D via stacked cross transformers and decodes the object pose in a fully decoupled end-to-end manner. 3.4 Training and Inference Protocol 

We train the proposed GenCOPE framework in an end-to-end manner, jointly optimizing semantic consistency learning and pose regression. The overall training objective consists of two components: semantic consistency losses that supervise the feature encoders, and a pose regression loss that guides the decoupled pose decoder. Specifically, the total training loss is defined as: 

Ltotal = LC_text -2D + LC_text -3D + Lpose , (7) where the first two items enforce semantic alignment between visual features and text-derived semantic priors within each object category, and Lpose supervises the regression of the object pose. 

Lpose is computed as the sum of the L2 norms of translation, rotation, and size prediction errors [34, 65], which supports the pose-decoupled decoder by supervising each pose component independently. 

During training , the semantic consistency losses LC_text -2D and LC_text -3D are applied to constrain the 2D and 3D encoders to focus on intra-class semantic invariants, thereby reducing reliance on domain-specific cues and improving robustness to domain shifts between synthetic and real-world data. The pose loss Lpose is jointly optimized to ensure accurate pose estimation. At inference time ,the semantic consistency supervision parts are removed. Given a single RGB-D image, the trained 2D and 3D encoders, 2D-3D cross consistency learning module, and pose decouple decoder are executed in a single forward pass to directly predict the full 9DoF object pose, without requiring any textual input or additional optimization. This design enables efficient and scalable deployment in real-world robotic applications. 

## 4 Experiments 

Datasets We follow previous COPE methods [59, 34, 65] to utilize the CAMERA25 [2], REAL275 [2], and Wild6D [59] datasets for evaluation. Specifically, we use the large synthetic CAMERA25 for training, which includes 1085 instances distributed in ∼250K synthetic images. During inference, we evaluate on real-world REAL275 and Wild6D datasets and self-collected robotic picking scenes. 

Implementation Details The input RGB-D images are captured at a resolution of 640 × 480 . For preprocessing, instance segmentation is first performed using Mask R-CNN [66]. The resulting object mask is applied to both the RGB and depth images, after which the RGB crop is resized to 192 × 192 

to reduce computational overhead. The temperature parameter τ in Eq. 2 is set to 0.07 as the previous contrastive learning method [69], the feature dimension d in Eq. 4 is fixed to 32, and the batch size 

b is set to 48. Model optimization adopts a cyclic learning rate schedule, where the learning rate oscillates between 1 × 10 −6 and 1 × 10 −4 using CyclicLR [71], with a step size of 20K iterations per cycle. Our model is trained for a total of 100 cycles. All experiments on public benchmarks are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU with 24 GB memory. Real-world experiments are deployed on a physical robotic platform equipped with an NVIDIA GTX 1660 GPU with 6 GB memory and an Intel RealSense L515 RGB-D camera. 

Evaluation Metrics Following common practice in prior work [42, 20, 54, 52, 65], we evaluate performance using the n◦mcm and 3D Intersection-over-Union ( IoU 3D) metrics. The n◦mcm 

criterion measures pose accuracy by jointly thresholding rotation and translation errors, where a prediction is deemed correct if the rotation and translation errors fall below n◦ and mcm , respectively. We report results under 5◦2cm , 5◦5cm , 10 ◦2cm , and 10 ◦5cm settings. The IoU 3D metric quantifies the overlap between the estimated and ground-truth 3D bounding boxes and is primarily used to assess size estimation accuracy. A prediction is considered correct when IoU 3D exceeds a threshold of 75 %. ## 5 Conclusion 

We presented GenCOPE, a simple yet effective Syn2Real generalized COPE method that enables models trained solely on synthetic data to generalize robustly to real-world deployment. We first analyze the root causes of domain discrepancies between synthetic and real-world data, and guided by these findings, introduced semantic consistency-guided 2D and 3D encoders and the 2D-3D cross consistency learning network with an end-to-end pose-decoupled decoder. Extensive results show that GenCOPE achieves superior domain generalization with minimal computational overhead and real-time inference, making it well-suited for practical integration into real-world robotic platforms. 

Limitation Although GenCOPE achieves encouraging results, the current formulation does not explicitly account for pose equivalence in strongly symmetric categories, where multiple rotations can describe the same physical configuration. Incorporating symmetry-aware losses and evaluation protocols remains an important direction for future work.

## B GenCOPE Guided Robotic Picking 

To validate the practicality of GenCOPE trained solely on synthetic data, we deploy it on a real-world eye-in-hand robotic picking platform equipped with an Intel RealSense L515 RGB-D camera and an onboard NVIDIA GTX 1660 GPU with 6 GB memory. Owing to its lightweight design, GenCOPE runs entirely on the embedded hardware without offloading computation. Representative pose estimation results are shown in the top-left of Fig. B. To ensure safe grasp execution, the 16 grasp position is manually offset by 2 cm above the tabletop, and the grasp orientation is tilted upward by 30°. Multiple objects in the scene, including textureless metallic components, are then sequentially picked based on their estimated center depths, as illustrated in Fig. B. These real-world experiments demonstrate the strong deployability, high efficiency, and robust Syn2Real generalization of GenCOPE, confirming its effectiveness for robotic picking. Time

The second column evaluates whether CLIP-based [37] text features are introduced during both training and inference via direct residual addition or feature concatenation, while the third column examines the effect of enabling the proposed 2D and 3D semantic consistency supervision during training. FC-2D and FC-3D. The experimental results in Table A show that directly injecting text features into the visual encoders does not consistently improve performance (Table A S0 and S1). Feature concatenation achieves performance comparable to our full model (Table A S3), whereas residual 18 addition slightly degrades (Table A S2). Moreover, introducing text features during both training and inference inevitably increases computational cost and memory consumption, which is undesirable for real-time robotic deployment. In contrast, incorporating the proposed semantic consistency supervision consistently improves performance, regardless of whether text features are explicitly fused into the 2D/3D representations. These results indicate that enforcing semantic alignment during feature learning is more effective than directly multimodal feature fusion. Overall, the findings validate that the proposed 2D/3D semantic consistency supervision enables the encoders to focus on domain-invariant semantic cues, thereby improving robustness to domain-specific information while maintaining computational efficiency and training stability. 19
